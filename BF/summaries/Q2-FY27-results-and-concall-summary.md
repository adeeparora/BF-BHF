## Main takeaway

Bajaj Finance delivered strong growth with improving underlying credit costs: consolidated AUM rose 26%, operating profit before provisions rose 23%, and reported PAT before minority interest rose 29%. Lower underlying provisions helped profit grow faster than income. The company also booked a ₹337 crore management and macroeconomic provision.

The main issues to monitor are the gold-loan Stage 2 increase, funding and margin pressure, regulatory uncertainty over revolving loans and insurance fees, and returns after the proposed capital raise. Management's confidence is a forward-looking assessment; it does not establish that these risks have been resolved.

### What drove the quarter

- AUM increased ₹37,680 crore during Q2.
- New loans booked were 13.45 million, up 11%. Festival timing affected the year-on-year comparison.
- Added 4.42 million customers; total franchise reached 128.85 million and the selected cross-sell franchise 82.36 million.
- Cost of funds declined 9 bps sequentially to 7.31%. Management attributed part of the quarterly benefit to interest-rate hedging.
- Liquidity buffer was ₹19,995 crore. Deposits were ₹69,754 crore, or 14% of consolidated borrowings.
- Gold-loan distribution reached 2,022 branches after 321 additions in Q2.
- On the call, management highlighted rapid growth in consumer finance and newer businesses, while MSME growth remained muted and the captive two-wheeler book continued to run down.

**Source:** Slides 5–7; call opening remarks and growth discussion.

### Profit growth: separate operating improvement from provisions

Underlying loan losses and provisions, excluding the ₹337 crore overlay, were ₹1,829 crore, down 12% year on year; underlying annualised credit cost was 1.32%. Reported provisions were ₹2,166 crore, up 4%.

The company presented PBT excluding the overlay of ₹8,874 crore and PAT excluding it of ₹6,619 crore, both up 34%. These are management-adjusted figures, not reported earnings.

**Interpretation:** This quarter's profit acceleration reflects both business growth and a credit-cost benefit. Operating leverage was modest year on year: expenses and net total income both rose approximately 23%, while the expense ratio improved 10 bps. The lower credit-cost comparison may not repeat at the same rate indefinitely.

**Source:** Slides 6–7 and 35; interpretation based on these figures.

### First-half context and standalone distinction

H1 FY27 consolidated net total income was ₹31,198 crore, up 23%; pre-provisioning operating profit ₹20,837 crore, up 22%; PBT ₹16,686 crore, up 29%; and PAT before minority interest ₹12,447 crore, up 28%. H1 opex / income was 33.2%, versus 33.1% a year earlier.

Bajaj Finance standalone Q2 AUM was ₹426,879 crore, up 26%; PAT ₹5,536 crore, up 30%; NII ₹12,150 crore, up 25%; ROA 5.4%; and ROE 20.2%. Its annualised reported credit cost was 2.09%. Do not compare this standalone credit-cost ratio directly with the consolidated 1.56% without allowing for subsidiary mix.

**Source:** Slides 35–36.

## 2. Asset quality: the key call discussion

### Gold-loan Stage 2 increase

Combined Stage 2 and Stage 3 assets increased ₹1,601 crore during Q2, including ₹1,047 crore in the gold-loan business linked to short-tenor schemes. The reported combined ratio increased to 2.02% from 1.87% in Q1.

Management said frequent maturity and rollover requirements under short-tenor products caused customers to enter delinquency. It stopped offering these schemes in August and favoured longer-tenor bullet loans, with optional periodic interest payments and early repayment flexibility.

On the call, management said roughly ₹550 crore of approximately ₹1,100 crore affected gold-loan Stage 2 balances had already returned to Stage 1. These call figures are approximate and refer to a subsequent resolution update; they should not be substituted for the presentation's ₹1,047 crore quarterly increase.

Management expects no credit-cost impact from this transition. Adjusting for the transient gold-loan movement, the presentation reports combined Stage 2 and Stage 3 at 1.88%, versus 1.90% in Q1.

**What to check next:** Actual cures of the remaining balances, fresh slippages, recoveries and auctions, credit costs, and growth under the revised product structure. A management expectation of no loss is not evidence of completed recovery.

**Source:** Slides 6–7; gold-loan Q&A.

### Other credit-quality observations

- Management said recent 3-, 6- and 9-month loan vintages had improved substantially.
- It attributed the elevated MSME NPA ratio principally to low denominator growth and said recent origination quality was strong.
- It described reducing exposure to customers with multiple personal loans: the call's “3PL” origination measure had fallen from roughly 14% to 7%, with an intention to halve it again. The supplied transcript does not give a precise formal definition of this measure.
- District-level monitoring had led to selective reductions in rural, MFI and B2B lending where weather-related stress was emerging, including parts of Maharashtra.
- Management argued that seasonal NPA comparisons should consider quarter length and combined Stage 2 plus Stage 3 movements.

**Source:** Asset-quality, consumer leverage and rural-business Q&A. These are management explanations, not independently tested conclusions.

### Why create an overlay when credit trends are improving?

Management described the overlay as a resilience buffer above modelled ECL, reflecting risks that historical models may not capture fully. It cited weather, energy prices and possible pressure on self-employed unsecured borrowers.

The call transcription gives approximate stocks of ₹226 crore macro provisions, ₹512 crore management overlays, and ₹350–500 crore additional provisioning associated with LGD floors. The transcript's quarterly overlay breakdown contains a numerical inconsistency; these figures are not reconciled here and should not be added mechanically. The authoritative reported Q2 management and macro provision is ₹337 crore.

**Source:** Provisioning-overlay Q&A; slide 6 for the exact quarterly provision.

## 4. Proposed capital raise and regulation

### Capital raise

The board approved, subject to shareholder approval, up to ₹11,700 crore through a QIP and up to ₹5,800 crore of convertible warrants to Bajaj Finserv: a total of up to ₹17,500 crore.

Management said the purposes were growth and resilience. Consolidated leverage was 5.1x; the company uses a 5.0–5.5x corridor for resilience and capital planning. BFL capital adequacy was 20.33%, with Tier 1 of 19.43%—these capital ratios are standalone.

On the call, management said an immediate BHF stake reduction could have supplied some capital, but it preferred to give BHF time to shift its mix from super-prime/prime toward prime/near-prime. It stated an eventual intention to retain 75%, compared with approximately 87% then.

**Interpretation:** More capital supports growth and resilience but can dilute per-share earnings and reduce near-term ROE until deployed. The eventual effect depends on issue price, warrant conversion, actual capital raised and growth returns; none is calculated here.

**Source:** Slide 8, slide 40 and capital-raise Q&A.

### Regulatory issues

- **Revolving loans:** Management described the rules as draft at the time of the call. Under a hypothetical complete ban, it anticipated a temporary AUM impact and marginal pressure on NIM and profitability. It planned alternative term-loan variants from 1 November.
- **Insurance distribution:** Management described the commission-related material as a discussion paper and was preparing scenarios. It deferred a decision on distribution architecture until final rules.
- These descriptions reflect the call date. This note does not verify subsequent regulatory developments or quantify exposure where the call did not.

**Source:** Regulatory and fee-income Q&A.

## 5. FINAI: execution progress, benefits still to be proven

The presentation reports 328 dedicated AI employees, 130 AI agents and 65 agentic deployments. AI voice and text bots handled 75% of DIY servicing. Disbursements from voice/text bot-generated leads reached ₹2,744 crore, up 112%.

Management described testing an AI-based customer risk model in B2B lending, with a planned sample of roughly 500,000 accounts. It cited modelled improvement of 15–20% in risk outcomes, subject to actual loan-vintage performance before wider deployment.

**Interpretation:** Distribution and workflow adoption are visible. A durable reduction in company-wide costs or credit losses attributable to AI is not yet separately demonstrated by these activity measures.

**Source:** Slides 9–13 and call opening remarks.

## 7. Questions for the next quarter

1. Does the remaining gold-loan Stage 2 pool cure without material losses, and does the revised product retain customers?
2. Does funding-cost pressure produce greater NIM compression than management currently anticipates?
3. Can BFL deliver expense-ratio improvement while expanding branches and AI investment?
4. How much credit-cost improvement comes from sustainable origination quality versus mix shifts and the captive-book run-off?
5. What are the final revolving-loan and insurance rules, the actual exposure, and the economics of replacement products?
6. What are the capital-raise terms and the resulting per-share earnings and ROE path?
7. Does BHF's near-prime/affordable expansion improve income growth while maintaining sound credit performance?
